
# Genesis 1:20–23 — The Fifth Day: Living Creatures in the Waters and Birds in the Air; Filling the Realms of the Heavens and the Waters

**New American Standard Bible (NASB):**

> 1:20 Then God said, “Let the waters teem with swarms of living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth in the open expanse of the heavens.” 
> 
> 1:21 And God created the great sea creatures and every living creature that moves, with which the waters swarmed, according to their kind, and every winged bird according to its kind; and God saw that it was good. 
> 
> 1:22 God blessed them, saying, “Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let birds multiply on the earth.” 
> 
> 1:23 And there was evening and there was morning, a fifth day.

## I. The Placement of This Passage within the Literary Structure of Chapter 1

### Mirror Correspondence

- ✅ Day 2: The establishment of the expanse (firmament) and the separation of the waters above from the waters below, creating the spatial realms of the waters and the air (the "stage").
- ✅ Day 5: The creation of marine life and birds, which inhabit the realms prepared on the second day—thereby filling these spaces (the living creatures upon the "stage").

### The first three days establish the realms; the subsequent three days fill them accordingly.

Day 1: Realms ↔ Day 4: Celestial Bodies | Day 2: The Expanse (Waters/Sky) ↔ Day 5: Aquatic Creatures and Birds | Day 3: Land and Vegetation ↔ Day 6: Land-dwelling Creatures and Humans

The fifth day marks the first instance where living entities are bestowed with the blessing to "be fruitful and multiply." This represents a pivotal narrative turning point: although the vegetation created earlier could reproduce via seeds, the text does not explicitly record God speaking a blessing over plants. With the creation of marine and aerial life on the fifth day, God’s explicit command of blessing emerges—establishing a literary and theological paradigm that paves the way for the blessing pronounced upon humanity on the sixth day (1:28).

Furthermore, the recurring phrase "according to their kinds" reinforces the principle of biological boundaries first introduced with the vegetation on the third day. Every living creature is assigned specific categorical limits by God, ensuring that the proliferation of life unfolds strictly within the order He has established.

## II. In-depth, Verse-by-Verse Analysis

### 1. "And God said, 'Let the waters teem with living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth across the expanse of the heavens.'"

God’s command points toward two distinct realms, corresponding precisely to the two spaces separated on the second day: the waters below and the expanse (the sky) above.

The Hebrew root for "teem" carries the connotation of "swarming" or "proliferating abundantly." This echoes the initial state, in which the great waters covered the entire earth. Now, by God’s command, these waters are no longer merely a medium for chaos; they have become a cradle for the proliferation of life. The very waters that once represented chaotic danger are transformed by the Creator into a space that sustains abundant life.

"Living creatures" (*nephesh*): This marks the first appearance in the Bible of the pivotal term *nephesh*—denoting a "living soul" or "living being"—which distinguishes these creatures from the plants mentioned on the third day. While plants possess the capacity for growth and reproduction, *nephesh* refers to living beings endowed with sentience, breath, and the ability to move independently; this represents an advancement in the hierarchy of life. This is not to say that plants lack life, but rather that the biblical text intentionally distinguishes between different levels of created life forms.

"Let birds fly above the earth across the expanse of the heavens": This precisely identifies the space designated as the "expanse" on the second day. The spatial framework across these chapters fits together seamlessly, revealing that Moses’ narrative structure is a meticulously designed whole, rather than a collection of disjointed stories.

### 2. "So God created great sea creatures (*tannînim*) and every living creature that moves, with which the waters teem, according to their kinds; and He also created every winged bird, according to its kind."

The term *tannînim* ("great sea creatures") is translated in many versions as "sea monsters" or "great beasts of the sea." In the mythology of the ancient Near East, such creatures often served as symbols of chaos—monsters that waged war against the gods. The Genesis narrative, however, offers a subversive reinterpretation: these great sea creatures are themselves the handiwork of God; they are not independent, chaotic forces of opposition. In pagan mythology, these monsters represent a chaotic threat, but here, they are merely one category of living marine creatures within God’s created order—fully subject to the Creator and possessing no power to stand against Him. This serves as an apologetic declaration that runs throughout the book of Genesis, dismantling the mythological systems of the ancient Near East that posited the existence of chaotic, divine-like adversaries.

The phrase "according to their kinds" is repeated twice—once for marine life and once for birds. God established stable boundaries for these living, moving creatures; the diversity of life is not an amorphous, fluid state, but rather a rich variety contained within the framework He has defined.

The verb used here remains *asah* ("made/created"), which—following the initial act of *bara’* (creating the substance of the heavens and the earth) in verse 1:1—refers to the subsequent work of bringing order to the created world and populating it with life.

### 3. "And God saw that it was good."

A singular verdict: Living creatures of the waters and the air, each formed according to its kind, are perfectly suited to the habitats God prepared for them. Having fulfilled the purpose of their creation, they are pronounced "good." Living beings are placed within their respective domains; the stage—comprising the firmament and the waters, established on the second day—is now filled with life.

### 4. "And God blessed them, saying, 'Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let birds multiply on the earth.'"

#### The Theological Pinnacle of This Passage: The Blessing (*barakh*) at the Level of Creation

To bless is for God to declare His benevolent will toward His creatures, bestowing upon them both the capacity and the mandate to multiply and fill the seas and the earth.

The structure here serves as a direct foreshadowing of the blessing pronounced upon humanity in verse 28: "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth." A progression is evident:

- The Fifth Day: Marine life and birds → Be fruitful and multiply; fill the seas and the earth.
- The Sixth Day: Humanity → Be fruitful and multiply; fill the entire earth and exercise dominion over all.

The logic of God’s blessing is as follows: He first grants created life the mandate to multiply and fill the earth, and only subsequently entrusts humanity with a higher level of authority—the responsibility to govern. The created order is endowed with the potential for abundant reproduction, awaiting the arrival of beings made in God’s image to exercise stewardship and dominion.

⚠️ **Important Distinction:** The blessing bestowed upon the living creatures on the fifth day is limited to "being fruitful and multiplying"; it does not include the granting of dominion. The authority to govern the earth is a unique mandate reserved exclusively for those created on the sixth day in God’s own image. There is a hierarchy of life, and each blessing carries a corresponding mission; not all living creatures receive the same calling.

### 5. "And there was evening, and there was morning—the fifth day."

The Hebrew logic of timekeeping remains consistent: moving from dusk toward the light of dawn. The narrative rhythm of each day reaffirms that the entire temporal order of creation is established solely by God.

## III. Synthesis: Connecting Core Motifs in Genesis 1–6

### 1. The Motif of Transforming Chaos

The "deep waters" mentioned in Genesis 1:2 serve as a vessel for chaos. On the second day, God sets boundaries for these waters; on the fifth day, He causes them to teem with abundant life. In the hands of the Creator, the very same "water" is transformed from a chaotic abyss into a dwelling place rich with life.

Later, the narrative of the Flood represents a reversal: God withdraws His constraints upon the waters, allowing them to once again become an instrument of judgment. Following the Flood, in the Noahic covenant, God re-establishes boundaries, enabling the earth to continue sustaining life.

This dual imagery of water—as both a medium for life and an instrument of chaotic judgment—permeates the entire book of Genesis.

### 2. The Progressive Motif of Blessing and Mission

The fifth day initiates the blessing paradigm of "being fruitful and multiplying," laying the groundwork for humanity’s mission.

A central thread throughout the history of redemption is that of the "offspring" (or "seed"): the offspring of the woman in Genesis 3:15, the line of Seth, Noah, the descendants of Abraham, and ultimately, Christ.

The blessing of "being fruitful and filling the earth"—given at the level of creation—is transformed after the Fall into the hope for a "promised offspring" within the framework of redemption. The origin of this thread lies in the blessing God bestowed upon living creatures on the day of their creation.

### 3. The Continued Motif of Boundaries: "According to Their Kinds"

From the plants on the third day, to the sea creatures and birds on the fifth, and finally the land animals on the sixth, the recurring phrase "according to their kinds" reinforces a core principle of creation: God is the Establisher of order and boundaries.

The tendency of sin is always to break through boundaries and blur categories; the narrative in Genesis 6 concerning the "sons of God" and the "daughters of men" serves as a foreshadowing of how the dissolution of boundaries leads to corruption.

### 4. Laying the Groundwork for the Imago Dei (Image of God) and the Mandate

Great sea creatures, birds, and all living things were created and blessed to multiply; their very existence awaited the arrival of a steward.

God first prepared a world teeming with life, then created humanity—made in His image—to exercise dominion. The order of grace is always this: God first prepares a rich, created environment, and only then entrusts the mandate of stewardship.

The world entrusted to human stewardship is not an empty wasteland, but a beautiful realm already blessed by God and filled with a diverse array of living creatures.

### 5. The Long-Range Typology of the Redemption of Sea Monsters

In the poetic and prophetic books of the Old Testament, great sea creatures often appear as symbols of chaos or as adversaries that God is able to subdue (e.g., Psalm 74; Isaiah 27). The foundation for this lies in the fifth day of Creation: even the most terrifying sea monsters were, in their origin, merely part of God’s good creation. Every force that appears formidable or threatening is ultimately subject to the sovereignty of the Creator.

## IV. Clarifying Common Misconceptions in Biblical Interpretation

### Pitfall 1: "The 'great fish' or 'sea monsters' are actually dinosaurs, proving that the Bible records the age of the dinosaurs."

**Analysis:** The semantic range of the term *tannînim* encompasses all manner of massive marine creatures; the text provides no specific paleontological classification. The focus of the passage is not to provide a catalog of prehistoric species, but rather to declare that even the largest and most fearsome sea creatures were created by God—they are not independent, chaotic forces. Becoming fixated on identifying specific prehistoric species can obscure the core theological proclamation of this passage.

### Pitfall 2: "The term *nephesh* ('living soul' or 'living being') applies only to animals and humans, implying that plants possess no life whatsoever."

**Analysis:** The Bible employs various terms to describe different forms of life, distinguishing between "living, sentient beings capable of breath and perception" and the vegetative life of plants; this does not mean that plants are devoid of life. On the third day, God declared the vegetation to be "good," affirming the inherent value of plant life. While the biblical terminology establishes a hierarchy, it does not support the conclusion that plants are "lifeless."

### Pitfall 3: "The blessing given on the fifth day is identical to the blessing given to humanity."

**Analysis:** While the sentence structures are similar, the dimensions of the assigned missions differ. The blessing for animals is limited to "being fruitful and filling their living space," whereas the blessing for humanity includes the additional covenantal mandate to "subdue and have dominion over the whole earth." The similarity in the phrasing serves as a literary device to highlight the unique, image-bearing mission of humanity—it does not imply that humans and animals share an equivalent calling.

### Pitfall 4: "The fact that birds were created from the waters suggests that the evolutionary theory of an oceanic origin of life aligns with the Bible."

**Analysis:** The biblical text commands the waters to "bring forth living creatures," while separately mentioning birds; it does not state that birds emerged from the water. Furthermore, the entire account is strictly governed by the constraint "according to its kind," which precludes any description of cross-kind evolution. Isolating the concept of "water bringing forth life" to support an evolutionary interpretation—while disregarding the boundary of "according to its kind"—amounts to taking the text out of context.

## V. Reflection for This Morning with God

The fifth day reveals that even the formidable and powerful sea creatures—which might appear dangerous—remain under God’s sovereign rule. The vast waters, once symbolic of chaos, can, at His command, teem with abundant life. No created power exists outside of His control.

Furthermore, this serves as a reminder that procreation and abundance are, in themselves, blessings from God. The continuation and flourishing of life are not mere accidents; rather, they are the result of the Creator’s intentional blessing. We often take the continuity of life for granted, forgetting that the emergence of each new generation is rooted in the word of blessing that God spoke at the dawn of creation.

### Questions for Reflection

Do I revere God’s sovereignty over all created life, acknowledging that no created power can ever surpass Him? Do I recognize that the very act of life’s proliferation is a gift from Him?

## VI. Summary and Preparation for the Next Section of Close Reading

Genesis 1:20–23 (the fifth day) completes the filling of the realms of the firmament and the waters—established on the second day. God causes the waters to teem with various living marine creatures and brings forth birds to fly across the firmament. The text reiterates the principle of biological boundaries—"according to their kinds"—and, for the first time, pronounces the blessing of "be fruitful and multiply" upon living creatures. This establishes both a literary and a theological paradigm for the creation and blessing of humanity on the sixth day.

The final link in the six-day mirrored structure is reached: the realm of dry land and vegetation (Day 3) corresponds to the realm of land-dwelling creatures and humans created in the image of God (Day 6) (Genesis 1:24–31).
